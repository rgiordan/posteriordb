# The Suffix "-gami" Across World Languages

The suffix "-gami" turns up in several unrelated languages, so its resonances come from a few separate sources.

## Japanese: rendaku on *kami*

In Japanese compounds, an initial *k* often voices to *g* (a process called *rendaku*), so the many words pronounced *kami* become "-gami" at the end of a compound. Each kanji carries a different meaning:

- **紙 (paper):** *origami* (folded paper), *kirigami* (cut paper), *tegami* (letter, literally "hand paper"), *chiyogami* (patterned decorative paper). This is the sense most familiar outside Japan.
- **神 (god, spirit):** *ujigami* (clan or local tutelary deity), *shinigami* (death god), *yamagami* (mountain god), *binbōgami* (god of poverty).
- **髪 (hair):** *kurokami* (black hair), *maegami* (bangs), *ushirogami* (hair at the back of the head). The idiom *ushirogami o hikareru* ("to have one's back hair pulled") means to leave reluctantly.
- **上 (above, upper, upstream):** common in surnames and place names such as *Ikegami* ("above the pond") and *Nogami*.
- **噛み (biting):** *amagami*, a gentle "sweet bite," like a puppy's playful nip.

So in Japanese, "-gami" can suggest paper and craft, the sacred, the body, or position, depending on the kanji.

## Sanskrit and its descendants: "going, moving toward"

In Sanskrit, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Nepali and related languages, *-gāmī* (गामी) comes from the root *gam*, "to go." It forms words for something that goes or tends in a direction:

- *agragāmī*: going ahead, a pioneer or vanguard
- *anugāmī*: following, a follower
- *dūragāmī*: far-reaching, as in far-reaching consequences
- *ūrdhvagāmī* / *adhogāmī*: rising upward / sinking downward, used physically and morally
- *āgāmī*: coming, upcoming. In Hindi and Bengali, *āgāmī* means "next" or "forthcoming," as in "next week."

In Buddhism (Pali and Sanskrit), the term has a spiritual meaning. A *sakadāgāmī* is a "once-returner," and an *anāgāmī* is a "non-returner," someone who will not be reborn into this world. Here the suffix evokes motion through time, direction, and destiny.

## Greek, via borrowing: "marriage, union"

From Greek *gamos* ("marriage") come words that many languages spell with *-gami*:

- Indonesian and Malay: *monogami*, *poligami*
- Danish, Norwegian and Swedish: *monogami*, *bigami*, *polygami*
- Turkish: *monogami*, *poligami*

English uses "-gamy" for the same words, and biology extends the sense to reproduction: *autogamy*, *exogamy*, *endogamy*.

## A nice coincidence

The Sanskrit *gam-* ("go") and the Greek *gamos* ("marriage") are not related. The Sanskrit root comes from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "come," which also gave English its word "come." The Greek root is usually traced to a separate root meaning "to marry" or "be related by marriage." The Japanese words are unrelated to either.

Taken together, "-gami" resonates with paper, spirit, hair, motion and direction, destiny, and union, depending on whose ears hear it.
